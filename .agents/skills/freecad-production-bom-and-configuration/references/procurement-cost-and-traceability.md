# Procurement, cost, and traceability

## Approved source control

An approved manufacturer part is not automatically available from every distributor. Store manufacturer and supplier identities separately. For each source record qualification status, site/region, quote date/expiry, lead time, MOQ, order multiple, packaging, and evidence.

Check lifecycle and counterfeit risk for electronic and safety-critical items. Supplier web pages are discovery evidence, not an immutable release record; archive the exact datasheet/certificate revision and hash when permitted.

## Alternate decision record

Assess at least:

- form, fit, function, performance, and tolerance;
- material/finish, environmental and shelf-life limits;
- interface pinout/protocol/firmware and configuration;
- regulatory/safety/customer approvals;
- manufacturing, tooling, inspection, service, and documentation;
- backward/forward interchangeability.

Record the resulting class: unrestricted alternate, conditional alternate, or bounded substitution/deviation.

## Cost basis

Never add unlike costs without normalizing:

- currency and exchange-rate date/source;
- quantity break and batch size;
- purchased versus manufactured UOM;
- landed versus ex-works basis;
- recurring versus non-recurring;
- quoted, standard, estimated, or actual;
- scrap/yield and rework assumptions.

Keep uncertainty or range for immature estimates. Report missing costs rather than treating them as zero.

## Traceability

Use lot tracking when items from one controlled batch may be treated together. Use serial tracking when individual history, calibration, life, warranty, or safety requires it. Store both when necessary.

Firmware/software records should include component identity, semantic or controlled version, checksum, target hardware, configuration/calibration data identity, load tool/process, and verification evidence. A filename alone is not a software baseline.

## Privacy and security

Use role-based access for commercial quotes, supplier documents, serial genealogy, and software hashes. Avoid personal data in BOM records unless required by the quality system. Follow the product cybersecurity process for firmware provenance and signing; this skill does not define one.
