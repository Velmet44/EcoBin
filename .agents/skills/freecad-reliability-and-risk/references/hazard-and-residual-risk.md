# Hazard analysis and residual risk

For machinery, ISO 12100:2010 describes lifecycle risk assessment and risk reduction; it remained current after confirmation in 2022, although revision work exists. Industrial robots and applications/cells are covered separately by ISO 10218-1:2025 and ISO 10218-2:2025. Confirm current editions, scope, regional law and applicable type-B/type-C standards.

## Lifecycle method

1. Define limits, intended use, foreseeable misuse and lifecycle states.
2. Identify hazards and sequences producing hazardous situations/events.
3. Estimate and evaluate initial risk using the controlled method.
4. Apply inherently safer design, then safeguards/protective measures, then information for use.
5. Verify implementation and effectiveness.
6. Check new hazards and changed operating states.
7. Estimate/evaluate residual risk and obtain the required human decision.
8. Communicate remaining risk, inspection, maintenance, training and PPE requirements where applicable.

Do not use warnings or PPE to compensate for a reasonably practicable inherently safer design or guard.

## Traceability

Each measure should link:

`hazard → requirement → design/process/control artifact → verification → residual-risk decision → information for use`

For safety-related control functions, reference the separately performed functional-safety analysis and its validated architecture. This skill must not infer a performance level, integrity level or diagnostic coverage from prose.

## Residual-risk approval

Approval records need the named person, authorized role, date, configuration/effectivity, decision, assumptions, open limitations and evidence reviewed. Self-approval by an unassigned role or an automated validator is not acceptable.

Any deviation must identify compensating controls, expiration/review date, affected serial/configuration range and release authority.
